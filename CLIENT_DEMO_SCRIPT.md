# Client Demo Script — Omni Monitor

**Length:** 25 minutes.
**Audience:** A prospect's CEO or COO. Decision-maker, technical-adjacent, evaluating internal execution tools.
**Goal:** convince them this is the layer their team needs between Slack chaos and traditional PM tools.

This is a turn-by-turn script. Use the role-specific demo user accounts you have set up in your demo workspace (`BRAND_CONFIG.demoModeBannerText` is a good place to flag the workspace as a demo if you want a visible banner).

---

## 0. Pre-demo checklist (do once, day before)

- [ ] Demo workspace seeded with ~20 tasks across 3 statuses (TODO, IN_PROGRESS, SUBMITTED, UNDER_REVIEW, BLOCKED).
- [ ] ~5 fake users: 1 CEO, 1 frontend Founder, 1 backend Founder, 4 interns (2 per dept).
- [ ] 3 templates created, 1 recurring task active.
- [ ] At least 1 AI report generated so the history shows real numbers.
- [ ] AI provider configured and tested — green "AI Active" pill expected.
- [ ] You're logged in as the CEO before the call starts.
- [ ] Browser zoomed to 110% so the prospect can see clearly on screen share.
- [ ] Mobile device handy for the §7 mobile demo.

---

## 1. Setup the problem (2 min — talking, no screen)

> "Most teams have two execution problems. First, work happens in Slack threads that nobody can find a week later. Second, the tools that try to fix it — Jira, Asana, ClickUp — are built for engineering project managers, not for the people actually doing the work and the executives reviewing it.
>
> Omni Monitor sits between those. It's an internal execution OS. Every task has an owner, a due date, a proof requirement, and a structured review flow. Every action is audited. Every leader gets a personalized dashboard. The AI summarizes execution health so you don't have to read every line.
>
> Let me show you what a Monday morning looks like for a CEO using it."

---

## 2. CEO Monday morning (5 min — screen share)

Open `/home` as CEO.

> "This is the CEO dashboard. Top of the page — the AI Executive Note. It's reading the actual state of the team right now and summarizing it: how many tasks are overdue, how many are in review, who's inactive, and what to do first. The green 'AI' badge means it was generated by the AI provider just now. If the AI was unreachable, it would fall back to a rule-based summary instantly — the user never sees a blank screen."

Click into the AI mode pill in the NavBar.

> "This green pill shows the AI is live. Customers who want to keep all inference on their own infrastructure can swap to a self-hosted Ollama endpoint just by changing one environment variable. No code redeploy."

Scroll down to "Operational snapshot" stats.

> "Six numbers at a glance. Overdue, needs-review, blocked, ideas, top score, total strikes. Below that — department health for frontend and backend independently. The CEO can see both."

Scroll to "Execution queues".

> "Three queues, one click each: tasks that are overdue, tasks waiting for review, tasks blocked. Notice the counts on each column. This is what an executive cares about — not the project timeline, but where execution is stalling RIGHT NOW."

Scroll to "AI critical insights".

> "These are rule-based + AI-flagged issues, ordered by severity. Inactivity, deadline misses, productivity drops, burnout risk. Advisory only — we never auto-discipline anyone. The CEO decides."

Scroll to rankings.

> "Top founders, top interns. We score on completed tasks, submissions, on-time delivery, and consistency. Strikes are subtracted. The CEO can apply strikes from the button at the top — but only for genuinely overdue work."

Click "Apply Strikes" on the demo. (Will report "No overdue strikes" — that's fine, narrate it.)

> "If anyone is overdue right now, this would apply strikes automatically and log every action in the audit trail. Today we're clean."

Stop here for questions.

---

## 3. The review workflow (4 min)

Switch to your Founder demo account in a separate browser profile.

> "Now I'm logged in as a Founder. The dashboard automatically scopes to my department only. I can't see the other department's data."

Open `/home` as Founder; show the scoped intern list.

> "Each intern in my department, with their current workload — overdue, pending review, blocked — visible at a glance. The AI Executive Note here is scoped to my department too."

Switch to an intern account.

> "And here's the intern view. Simpler. No company-wide stats, no rankings, no insights. Just today's work, deadlines, what's awaiting their reviewer, and what feedback they've received."

Click a SUBMITTED task → submit a proof from the intern view.

> "Submitting a proof is a structured action — a note, optionally a screenshot, and a checkbox to mark it as the formal submission. This moves the task into the review queue."

Switch back to the Founder account.

Open `/more` → Notifications. Click "Open task" on the proof-submitted notification.

> "The Founder gets a notification, clicks 'Open task', and the app jumps straight into the task with the reviewer view ready. They can Approve, Request Changes, or Reject — each with a comment."

Approve the task.

> "Approved. The intern gets a notification. The activity timeline records it. The audit log records it. Every state transition is searchable."

---

## 4. AI Reports (3 min)

As CEO, open `/more`. Click "Weekly AI Report".

> "This is what a Monday-morning report looks like. Rule-based insights from the operational data — task risk, deadline miss, inactivity, productivity trend, burnout risk, proof quality, department performance. The AI provider summarizes it into a paragraph at the top. Downloadable as JSON for further analysis."

Click Download JSON.

> "Customers can plug this straight into BI tools, share it with their board, or feed it back into their own analytics pipeline."

---

## 5. Admin diagnostics + audit log (3 min)

As CEO, scroll down `/more` to "Admin diagnostics". Click "Show".

> "This is the operational health screen. CEO-only — every other role gets nothing here. Failed AI reports in the last 30 days, tasks stuck in review for over a week, tasks waiting for a reviewer for over five days, overdue work, paused recurring tasks, and a 24-hour audit heartbeat to confirm the system is alive."

> "And below it — the audit log. Every important action, who did it, what they touched, when. Filterable by action type. Immutable. This is what an enterprise compliance team or a board audit asks for."

Filter by "TASK_REVIEW" to demonstrate.

> "Search for any action type. Click a row to see the full meta payload. Today, this is over 22 distinct action types."

---

## 6. Templates + recurring (2 min)

Open `/tasks` → Templates tab.

> "Routine work gets templates. Weekly standup notes, monthly board prep, daily ops check — define it once, assign it on a recurring schedule, the system creates the task instances automatically."

Click into a template; show recurrence options.

> "Daily, weekly on specific days, or one-shot. With an end date or open-ended. Templates can be private to one user, shared in a department, or company-wide."

---

## 7. Mobile (1 min)

Pull out your phone. Show the same `/home` page on mobile.

> "Everything works on a phone. Dashboard collapses to a single column. Interns can submit a proof from the bus. Executives can apply strikes between meetings. Same data, same RBAC, same audit trail."

## 8. Setup story (2 min — talking)

> "Deployment is Vercel for the front-end plus Supabase Postgres for the data. White-label branding through one config object today; per-workspace branding when we land the multi-tenant phase. AI provider is plug-and-play — OpenAI or your own self-hosted Ollama, environment variable swap. The whole thing is a few hundred dollars a month at small scale and scales linearly with usage.
>
> Day-one rollout for a 20-person company is about an afternoon — set up users, define a few templates, point AI at OpenAI, train the team for 30 minutes. That's it."

> "Three questions I get asked at this point. First: 'Can we self-host?' Yes — everything except the AI provider is your own infrastructure if you want it. Second: 'Can we integrate with our existing Jira / Asana?' Not today; we're focused on being the canonical execution layer rather than a Jira clone. Third: 'When can we start?' I can have a demo workspace for your team within 24 hours. Want me to set that up?
>
> If you're interested, the next step is a 14-day trial in your own workspace, no payment info required. We seed it with your real org structure, your team logs in, and we measure execution lift over two weeks. After that you decide.
>
> What questions can I answer?"

## 10. Common objections + how to handle

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "Looks like Jira lite." | "Jira is for shipping software; Omni Monitor is for shipping work. No epics, no sprints. Just tasks with proofs, reviews, and execution analytics." |
| "Our team already uses Notion." | "Notion is a wiki and a database. It does not enforce a review workflow, generate execution analytics, or have an audit trail. We're orthogonal — keep using Notion for docs, use Omni Monitor for execution." |
| "What about Slack integration?" | "Not in the current release. Notifications today are in-app only. Slack-relay is on the roadmap for the paid-pilot quarter. We can discuss webhook integration in v1.5." |
| "Can the AI see our private data?" | "Only what you tell it to see. If you configure your own Ollama endpoint, no data leaves your infrastructure. If you use OpenAI, you sign the standard OpenAI DPA. Either way, the AI is advisory — it never writes to your data." |
| "Pricing?" | "Free for under 5 users so you can validate; $79 / month for Starter; $249 / month for Pro; Enterprise is custom. I can send the full pricing matrix after the call." |
| "Compliance / SOC 2 / GDPR?" | "We're SOC 2 Type II in progress for next quarter. GDPR-ready data export is on the same roadmap. For enterprise customers we'll sign DPAs today." |

## 12. Tweak the demo for your buyer

If the buyer is engineering-led: spend more time on §5 (audit log, admin diagnostics, RBAC).
If the buyer is ops-led: spend more time on §2 + §3 (Monday morning + review workflow).
If the buyer is founder/CEO of a small startup: spend more time on §6 (templates + recurring) — they care about leverage.
If the buyer is enterprise: emphasize §4 + §8 (AI reports + setup) — they care about compliance + integration.

Cut anything that isn't landing. The script is a starting point, not a contract.
